Causes of Depression

Biological and Genetic Factors

  • Family history: You have a higher chance of getting depressed if close blood relatives also have the condition.

  • Brain function: Areas of the brain that manage mood may not work as well as they should.

  • Hormones Shifts in hormone levels during pregnancy, postpartum periods, thyroid issues, or menopause can trigger symptoms

  • Medical health: Long-term illnesses like chronic pain, heart disease, or diabetes raise your risk.

Life Events and Environment

  • Trauma and loss Physical abuse, sexual abuse, or the death of someone close can bring on deep sadness.

  • High stress: Ongoing money worries, job loss, or a bad relationship put heavy strain on your mind.

  • Loneliness: Long-term isolation and lack of social support make it hard to cope with daily life.

    Personal Traits and Habits

  • Personality style: Being very self-critical, worrying a lot, or having low self-esteem can make you more vulnerable.

  • Substance use: Heavy use of alcohol or recreational drugs often worsens or triggers depressive states.
